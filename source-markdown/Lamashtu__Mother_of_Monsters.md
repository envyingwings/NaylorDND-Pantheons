```columns
The Mother of Monsters is older than the current order of the Abyss, an Obyrith queen who remembers the Tanar'ri before they had shape or purpose, and who pressed both into a fraction of them to make the first demon lords. Gnolls, goblins, the deformed, and the discarded worship Lamashtu not for healing of these forms, but for the conviction that what the world names an abomination is simply something it does not yet have the courage to understand. Her faithful seek to spread that understanding by force if necessary, and mutation is not a curse in her theology but a gift still working.

Lamashtu rules from Kurnugia, the 366th layer of the Abyss, a realm of warped terrain and ancient warrens where her children are born and remade without end.
```

### Description
```columns
id: -Lamashtu-Description
===
Pictures of Lamashtu portray her as a jackal- or hyena-headed woman with a third vertical eye in the centre of her forehead, heavily pregnant, with ragged black feathered wings, a serpent’s tail, and vulture-like taloned feet. Her belly is often swollen, ringed by crude fetishes of bone and tooth, suggesting an endless, monstrous fertility. In more esoteric depictions from Abyssal cults, she wields a pair of curved blades said to echo her legendary weapons Redlust and Chillheart, dripping with phantom blood that never dries.

In many Abyssal mosaics and cult icons, her head shifts to match the worshiper: jackal or hyena among gnolls, serpent among medusae, hawk among harpies. She crouches over fouled rivers or fields of warped beasts, her three eyes weeping blood or black milk, claws sunk into the backs of writhing, half-formed creatures. These artworks emphasize her dual nature as both devouring predator and “mother”—jaws split in a jackal grin, arms wide in a mockery of embrace, surrounded by malformed children who cling to her and to each other.

More abstract shrines reduce her form to a stylized jackal head with three eyes, etched into stone or carved from bone. To her followers, any three-eyed animal skull, especially that of a jackal or hyena, is an acceptable holy symbol.
```

#### Celebrations and Worship Practices
Lamashtu is not usually worshiped in constructed temples, but at sites where boundaries between “civilized” and “wild” blur: a ring of standing stones, a grove of twisted trees hung with fetishes, a circle of stacked bones, a pit in the ground, or a broad, flat rock overlooking carrion-strewn gullies. Such places are typically found on the outskirts of civilization or in the deep wilderness, stained with old blood and littered with the remains of failed births and the victims of successful ones.

Shrines are decorated with deformed animal skulls, claw-raked stones, and crude carvings of jackal-headed women. Offerings of offal, spoiled grain, and vermin are common, symbolizing her dominion over famine, rot, and pestilence. Sacrifices of all life are welcomed to the goddess, whether it is her own abyssal offspring, a fecund commoner or noble, or at worst the sacrifice of newborn infants born without obvious flaw.
#### Commandments of Lamashtu
- Never change that which makes you different
- Make the beautiful monstrous, and the monstrous beautiful
- Reveal the corruption and flaws in all things
### History in Ouros
- The Obyrith Mother was one of the original demon princes of the Abyss, born from its raw, bestial madness long before the [[Tharizdun, The Chained Oblivion|Chained Oblivion]] laid claim to the plane. In that age, the Abyss seethed with nightmare shapes with no names and no order; Lamashtu rose from this chaos as a towering, jackal-faced horror who delighted in giving form to the formless. When the Abyss was later swollen and scarred by the influence of Tharizdun, she refused to bow, instead bending the new chaos to her own design.
- When the tanar’ri began to spawn from the Chained Oblivion’s corrupted essence, Lamashtu saw in them raw material rather than rivals. She tested and tormented these new demons, searching for those whose cruelty and will pleased her. Those she deemed worthy were reshaped by her blessing — fused, swollen, or refined into forms terrible enough to rule layers of the Abyss. In Ourosi myth, many of the most infamous Demon Princes trace their “second birth” to her claws, and even those who deny her claim cannot easily refute the resemblance.
- One origin myth of Lamashtu’s apotheosis whisper of an older god of beasts who once held dominion over all natural animals. Lamashtu ambushed this god in a remote corner of the Abyss, tearing from him the portfolio of beasts and warping it through her own deranged vision. That theft of bestial divinity, combined with the raw might she already possessed as an Obyrith queen, elevated Lamashtu beyond the rank of demon prince to that of goddess.
### Appendix
#### Abyssal Lords — Demon Princes of the Abyss
Many of the more infamous demon princes of the Abyss are spoken of in Ourosi lore as Lamashtu’s “second-born”: demons she tormented, tested, and reshaped until they were terrible enough to rule layers of the Abyss in their own right. These beings are not truly subordinate to her, but their legends often contain a chapter of brutal “rebirth” at her claws, and some are honored as her exarchs in monstrous cults.

## Known Organizations
- **Concubines of Second Birth.** The Concubines of Second Birth preserve worthy demons and champions through a dark rite. On the eve of battle, a warrior’s soul is bound to a concubine. If the warrior falls, their soul fuses with her, gestating within until rebirth as a Demon. The concubine temporarily gains aspects of the warrior’s strength but is drained by each birth, their soul withering over time. Despite their inevitable demise, the concubines willingly embrace this fate, venerating Lamashtu and ensuring no worthy soul is lost to the grave.
## Divine Relics
- **Ikathi di Televara, the Siblingsword.** This jagged, whispering weapon causes demons to imprint upon its bearer as if they were an honored sibling worthy of following. As long as the wielder sheds blood in Lamashtu’s name, the blade can wrench their “siblings” across planar boundaries, summoning demons from the Abyss to serve as a howling brood under their control. Hand-made by Lectus Arl, deceased champion of Lamashtu, from a broken fragment of the Rock of Oblivion.
- **Rock of Oblivion.** A dense, light-devouring shard torn from the deepest layers of the Abyss during Nerigoth’s War. The Rock of Oblivion acts as a knife to reality: when awakened, it tears open the planes around it, allowing demons to pour through in chaotic waves. In Hemor, it once served as the beating heart of Lamashtu’s war-machine, sustaining the demonic legions of the Magus General until it was sealed away.
