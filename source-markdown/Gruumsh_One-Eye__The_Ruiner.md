**Gruumsh** (pronounced: /gruːmʃ/ GROO-msh), known as **One-Eye** and **the Ruiner**, is the Ourosi god of orcs, slaughter, natural disasters, outcasts, curses, and destructive fury. To his faithful, the world is a kill-or-be-killed hunting ground locked forever in a neolithic dusk, where only raw strength and ruthless will deserve to endure. As undisputed ruler of the orcish pantheon, Gruumsh drives his children to multiply, raid, and burn, despising walls, laws, and comforts as lies that shelter the weak from the trials they deserve. Earthquakes, landslides, volcanic fire, and plague are preached as his scouring gifts—great culls that tear down the soft and leave only the hard, the hungry, and the worthy standing.

### Description

===
Simple clay or carved idols in communities of the Ruiner’s followers show him as a hulking, bulbous behemoth of an orc in jagged armor. His missing eye has shifted; the remaining eye sits dead center in his face like that of a cyclops.

Among orcs, Gruumsh is rarely shown in calm stillness. Charcoal daubs and carved idols depict him as a broad-shouldered, scar-furrowed orc with a single glaring eye burning in the center of his face, jaw split in a permanent snarl. His armor is crude but heavy, hammered from scavenged plate and stone; in some tribes he is shown half-bare, muscles knotted like tree roots, to emphasize raw strength over worked metal. His spear is jagged and barbed like broken bone, and his cloak is a mass of flayed hides and matted fur.

### History in Ouros
- In ancient battle, Corellon Larethian shot out Gruumsh’s right eye. Where the Ruiner’s blood fell, orcs and their kin arose; where his rage struck the land, the earth itself buckled and broke. Gruumsh swears one day to return this insult “twofold,” by blinding Corellon and drowning elven realms in ruin.
- Like Corellon, Gruumsh lays claim to a kind of rebirth. But where elves reincarnate to grow in wisdom, orcs are told that rebirth comes only through destruction: old things must be broken—cities, dynasties, customs, even tribes, so that something fiercer can rise from the wreckage.
- Gruumsh despises fortifications and ordered states. He teaches that cities deny orcs their rightful rulership by hiding the weak behind stone, steel, and sorcery. Any orc who seeks the safety of such places over the tests of the wild is said to have turned from One-Eye’s gift; in legend, these “soft ones” inevitably die screaming when the walls finally fail.
- His authority over curses manifests in blights, withering hexes, and plagues sent against those who “cheat the trial” through cowardice, treachery, or decadent ease. In many tales these afflictions are carried out through Yurtrus the White-Handed, his silent exarch of disease and decay. Orcs bear a visible stain of their god’s harsh mercies: when illness takes them, their hands pale like those of Yurtrus, the White-Handed. No orc can convincingly lie about their health; the color of their skin betrays them. In the sanctuaries of Luthic, orcs born frail or sickly are given over not to the Cave-Mother’s shamans but to Yurtrus’s white-robed cult, to be raised among the silent keepers of plague and rot.
- Gruumsh’s wrath manifests in his curses which invoke continent-shaking calamities sent against those who “cheat the trial” through cowardice, decadence, or false strength. Many Gruumite wise men teach that a **Curse of the Ruiner** lies in the blood of all orckind, bringing doom upon any leader who makes their people weak. The most dreaded tale speaks of an ancient orcish nation on a lost, metal-rich continent, where streets were lined with gold and windows shone with cut gems. Because this wealth was won without struggle or dominance, Gruumsh’s ire grew with their comfort. The rulers cloaked the land in wards to hide it from One-Eye, but one night the magic faltered. Gruumsh saw, and in a single curse turned every living thing on the continent to stone, smothering its jewel-bright ruins beneath a devouring jungle and erasing the kingdom and its shame forever.

## Known Organizations

The Cult of the Ruiner exists in wild and untamed lands, with many nomadic tribes having a shaman to the Ruiner.

- **Ruiner’s Horde.** A loose term for the great war-hosts that periodically form under a single vision or charismatic Eye of Gruumsh. These hordes often arise in the wake of natural disasters—earthquakes, eruptions, plagues—which are taken as signs that the time has come to sweep aside weakened realms. Each horde is short-lived but devastating, burning out only when internal challenges finally kill its war-leader or rival gods intervene.
- **Eyes of One-Eye.** The scattered network of Eyes of Gruumsh across Ouros, who occasionally convene in blood-soaked moot-sites to decide whether a rising war-chief truly holds the god’s favor or must be tested by ordeal.

## Divine Relics
- **Ruin’s Wake.** An infamous axe and Arm of the Betrayers, Ruin’s Wake appears as a jagged, cleaver-like weapon that seems permanently wet with old blood. In legend, its blows topple walls as easily as warriors, and on the eve of a great raid its wielder dreams of a single blazing eye showing them which settlements are ripe for slaughter. In Ouros, Ruin’s Wake is said to reappear whenever a horde grows strong enough to threaten an age of relative peace.

- **Battle Line**
	**Battle Line**, 2nd-level abjuration  
	**Casting Time:** 1 action  
	**Range:** 60 feet  
	**Components:** V, S, M (a pinch of sand)  
	**Duration:** Concentration, up to 1 hour
	You trace a visible line on the ground up to 30 feet long within range. When a creature crosses the line for the first time, it must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw or become frightened of you for the duration. At the end of each of its turns, a frightened creature can repeat the saving throw if it cannot see or hear you, ending the effect on a success.

- **Pocket Cave**
	**Pocket Cave**, 5th-level conjuration  
	**Casting Time:** 1 action  
	**Range:** 30 feet  
	**Components:** V, S, M (a pinch of dirt)  
	**Duration:** 8 hours
	You create an extradimensional cave within a surface of rock or earth that you can see within range. The entrance appears as a dark cave opening 4 feet wide and 8 feet tall. Only creatures you designate when you cast the spell can enter. The entrance seals and becomes invisible once you enter, but you can open it again from inside at will.
	The cave's interior is a dark, damp cavern large enough to comfortably shelter up to ten creatures. It contains enough breathable air for all creatures within for the duration. Edible fungus grows along the walls, and fresh water pools along one side, providing sufficient food and water for up to ten creatures per day. External weather and environmental conditions do not affect the interior.
	When the spell ends, all creatures within the cave are transported to the nearest unoccupied space outside the entrance.
	**Classes:** Cleric, Druid
